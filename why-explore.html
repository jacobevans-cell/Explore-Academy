Why Explore Peoria

A school should be more than a schedule of classes. It should be a place where students are known, challenged, safe, and able to discover new interests.

Our Difference

Small by design. Personal by nature.

Explore Peoria’s size creates something larger schools often have to work much harder to achieve: familiarity. Students and staff know one another. Families can form real relationships with the people teaching their children. Students have room to lead instead of disappearing into the crowd.

Small does not mean fewer opportunities. At Explore Peoria, the goal is the opposite: use a smaller community to make more opportunities reachable for more students.
What Families Gain

The value of being known.

Relationships

Teachers can know students as people, not just names on a roster. That makes support, accountability, and celebration more personal.

Belonging

A close-knit campus gives students repeated chances to connect with peers and adults across academics, clubs, events, athletics, and trips.

Access

Clubs during the school day, expanding sports, and frequent experiences help more students participate without extra transportation or program fees becoming the first barrier.

Campus Heritage

A community that did not begin yesterday.

Before Explore Academy Peoria opened in 2024, this same Peoria campus operated as Sonoran Science Academy Peoria. Historical records identify the same 17667 N 91st Ave location, and archived materials show a culture that included student clubs and family involvement. Explore is building a new academic identity while preserving the value of a close school community.

Then: Sonoran Science Academy Peoria

A long-running Peoria charter campus with a STEM focus, student activities, and a community familiar to many local families.

Now: Explore Academy Peoria

A growing K–12 pathway built around choice-based seminars, mastery, college preparation, technology, clubs, athletics, and experiential learning.

“Every student should have more than one way to succeed, more than one way to participate, and more than one adult who knows them well.”
